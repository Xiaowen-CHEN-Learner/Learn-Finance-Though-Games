XOM Trading Game — Round 1 of 5

Date: April 20, 2020 — market close
Exxon Mobil (XOM): $41.18
XOM fell 4.7% that day.

What you know at this moment

  • The COVID-19 shutdown has caused an extraordinary collapse in transportation and petroleum demand. Earlier in April, the EIA was forecasting global liquid-fuels demand in April to be about 17.1 million barrels/day below the 2019 average.
  • Today, the expiring May WTI crude contract collapsed to -$37.63/barrel, the first negative settlement in history. Storage constraints and evaporating near-term demand were major factors. Importantly, June WTI remained around $20.43, so the negative price was partly a specific futures/storage dislocation rather than "all oil is worth -$37."
  • Exxon had already responded on April 7 by cutting planned 2020 capital spending 30%, from $33 billion to about $23 billion, and targeted a 15% reduction in cash operating expenses.
  • There is one major potential positive: on April 12, OPEC+ agreed to cut crude production by 9.7 million barrels/day beginning May 1, an unprecedented attempt to rebalance the market.

The tension

The obvious textbook argument is:

Oil collapse → lower upstream earnings/cash flow → bad for XOM.

But you also know:

XOM has already fallen sharply + spending is being cut + OPEC+ is responding + the negative WTI price partly reflects an expiring-contract/storage problem.

Round 1 Result & Analysis

Decision Quality vs. Outcome Quality

The Outcome: In the short term following April 20, 2020, the panic subsided slightly as June contracts rolled over without going negative, and OPEC+ cuts began. By early June 2020, XOM rallied briefly back to the $50s, but then languished in the $30s through the fall of 2020 as the pandemic dragged on, before eventually beginning a massive multi-year bull run in 2021.

The Evaluation: If you chose to buy, you recognized that the -$37 oil price was an anomaly of futures expiration, not a permanent state, and that the market had already priced in maximum pessimism. If you chose to do nothing, you made a rational choice to avoid catching a "falling knife" during unprecedented macroeconomic uncertainty.

Lesson: A perfectly rational decision based on available information can still result in short-term pain. We evaluate your play based on whether you weighed the knowns vs. unknowns properly, not just on what the chart did next.
